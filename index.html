What it is

Each idea is one concrete thing you can run in a lesson or set for homework. It tells you what to do, what to type or paste, and what the students actually do. Most take minutes to set up.

The part that matters most: every idea comes in three versions, depending on whether a student is currently sitting at an A, a B or a C. They are genuinely different approaches, not just more of the same for the weaker group.

Finding yours

Open the link and pick your subject from the filter at the top. Every subject we teach is there, including French and Spanish, the community languages, Core Maths and the EPQ. If you would rather scan a table than read cards, switch to Grid view.

You can also filter by teaching approach. Pick a Rosenshine principle, say Provide models or Check for understanding, and see only the ideas that use it. Every idea names the principles it rests on and links the research behind them.

Also worth a look

  • What your exam board actually rewards at the top, with real grade boundaries. The A* mark ranges from about 70% in some subjects to 94% in others, so a single “aim for 90%” target misleads most departments.
  • A SEND adaptation on every idea.
  • A straight account of what the research does and does not support. Several popular techniques have weaker evidence than people assume, and it says so rather than selling them.

Two honest caveats. The ideas were checked against what the tools can really do, but they have not been trialled in our classrooms. Check anything against your specification before you use it.

And the obvious one: if the tool does the thinking, it is working against the A*. Every idea is built so the student does the work.
